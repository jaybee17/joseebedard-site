What Is a Brain File, and Why Does Claude Need One?

Most people ask Claude for a post and get something that sounds like every other AI post. The fix is not a better prompt. It is a brain file.

By Josée Bédard · November 16, 2026

Why AI writing sounds like a robot

When you open Claude and ask for a post, it knows nothing about you. It does not know your story, who you help, or how you talk. So it writes the safest, most average thing it can. That is the "AI voice" you have probably seen: smooth, friendly and the same as everyone else.

The problem is not the AI. It just does not know you yet.

What a brain file is

A brain file is one document, written in plain words, that tells Claude who you are. I save mine inside a Claude project, so every new chat in that project already knows me. I no longer have to explain my business each morning.

Every one of my AI helpers reads my brain file before it writes a word. It is the base that everything else sits on.

What goes in a brain file

1. Your story

Not your resume. The real beats: where you started, what went wrong, what you figured out. Mine says I was a mail carrier. It says I burned out. That is why my content does not sound like a guru.

2. Who you help

The kind of person, and the problem they have. Be specific. "Women over 50 who feel stuck and want a small online business" works better than "everyone".

3. What you offer

A few simple lines. What it is, who it is for, what it helps with.

4. How you talk

Short sentences? Plain words? Kitchen-table talk? A little humor? Write it down the way you would explain it to a friend.

5. Words you never use

This is the part almost nobody does, and it is what removes the robot voice. List the hype words, the guru words and the words that sound like a seminar. If you would never say it out loud, ban it.

6. What you never promise

For example: no promises about money, no medical claims, no "guaranteed results". Putting this in writing protects you.

How to make one in about 20 minutes

You do not write it from scratch. You ask Claude to interview you, one question at a time. Then it writes it up for you.

Step 1: Open a project and start a chat

In Claude, create a new project and call it "My Brain File". Start a chat inside it.

Step 2: Paste this

Interview me for 20 minutes so you can learn to write as me.
Ask me one question at a time about: my story, who I help,
what I sell, how I talk, and the words I would never use.
When we are done, write it all up as one file I can save.

Step 3: Answer like you are talking to a friend

Do not polish your answers. The rough version of you is the one that works. If you would say "ugh, I hate it when people say that", say it. That is gold for a brain file.

Step 4: Tell your story, not your resume

When it asks about you, skip job titles. Tell the real beats: where you started, what went wrong, what you figured out.

Step 5: Give it your banned words

Paste this near the end of the interview:

Here are words and phrases I never use: [your list].
If you ever write one of them, you have broken character.
Also: short sentences. Plain words. Kitchen-table talk.

Step 6: Save it where it always gets read

When the interview is done, Claude writes up your brain file. Save it in the project's instructions, or as a file in the project. Now every new chat in that project starts already knowing you.

What a finished brain file looks like

Yours will be different, but here is the shape of one:

About me: [two or three lines of your real story]

Who I help: [your people and their main problem]

What I offer: [a few simple lines]

How I sound: short sentences, plain words, warm and honest.

Words I never use: [your list]

Things I never promise: money, results, anything medical.

Test it, then correct it once

Ask Claude to write a short post about something real. Read it out loud. Anything that does not sound like you, say so, and add the correction to the file:

That line does not sound like me. I would say it like this: [your version].
Add that as a permanent rule in my brain file.

Every correction makes the file smarter. After a week of this, you will read a draft and forget you did not write it.

Keep it fresh

  • Once a month, read it and update anything that changed.
  • When you notice a new word you hate, add it to the banned list.
  • When you launch something new, add it to "What I offer".

Common mistakes

  • Writing it like a resume. Claude does not need your job titles. It needs your voice.
  • Making it too long. A page or two is plenty. Short and true beats long and fuzzy.
  • Skipping the banned words. This is the single biggest fix for robot voice.
  • Putting private details in. Leave out passwords, bank details and anyone else's private information.
  • Never testing it. Always run a real example and correct it.

Frequently asked questions

How long does it take?

About 20 minutes for the interview, and a few more minutes to test and fix it.

Do I need to pay for Claude?

You can start on a free plan and see how it feels. Plans and limits change, so check claude.com/pricing for the latest.

Can I use it with other AI helpers?

Yes. A brain file is just words. You can paste it into any AI helper, though I build mine inside Claude.

Will it make the AI sound exactly like me?

Close, not perfect. That is why I always read and correct. It gets closer each week.

What is the difference between a brain file and a prompt?

A prompt is one request. A brain file is who you are, saved once and used in every request.
