What Is an AI Employee? (Plain Words, No Tech)

I call my AI helpers "employees," and people ask me what that means. Here it is in plain words, with a full example you can copy.

By Josée Bédard · November 16, 2026

An AI employee is a helper with one job

Think about hiring a person. You would not say, "Do everything." You would say, "Your job is this one thing, and here is how I like it done."

An AI employee is the same idea. It is an AI helper, like Claude, that you give one job and clear instructions. It does that job the same way every time, so you do not start from zero each morning.

That is all it is. There is no magic and no code.

Chat, project, employee: what is the difference?

This is where most beginners get confused, so here it is in three steps:

  • A chat. You open Claude and ask for something. When the chat ends, it forgets you.
  • A project. You save your instructions and notes in one place. Every new chat in that project already knows you.
  • An AI employee. A project with one clear job, written down, that you use over and over.

So an AI employee is really a project with a job description. That is why it feels like a coworker, not a search box.

What my AI employees do

I run my business with thirteen of them. Each has one small job. One writes a first draft of a post. One helps plan my week. One helps with pictures. They do not replace me. They do the first pass, and I check and approve it.

I think of it as a 90/10 split. The AI does about 90 percent of the first draft. I do the last 10 percent, the part that needs my judgment and my voice. That 10 percent is the part nobody else can do for me.

Eight jobs you could give your first AI employee

  1. Write a first draft of a weekly post or email
  2. Turn your rough notes into a clean guide
  3. Plan your week from a messy to-do list
  4. Write replies to common questions, for you to check
  5. Make a list of ten ideas every Monday
  6. Rewrite something so it is shorter and simpler
  7. Make a checklist from a long explanation
  8. Prepare questions to ask before you start a new project

Pick the one you like the least. That is the job to hand off first.

What an AI employee is not

  • It is not magic. It makes mistakes, so you always read it before it goes out.
  • It is not a person. It does not know your business until you tell it.
  • It does not run things for you with no help. You are still the boss.
  • It is not an expert on your private life. Do not paste passwords, bank details or private information about other people into it.
  • It can be wrong about facts. If a fact matters, check it yourself.

How to build your first one, step by step

Step 1: Pick one job

Choose the job you like the least. Not three jobs. One.

Step 2: Teach it who you are

This is where most people get stuck, and the fix is a short interview. The result is a short document I call a brain file. I wrote it all up in What Is a Brain File?.

Step 3: Write its job description

Say what the job is, what a good result looks like, and what to never do. Here is a simple template you can copy and fill in:

You are my [job name]. Your one job is to [what it does].

Who I help: [your people].
How I talk: short sentences, plain words, like I am talking at a kitchen table.
Always: [what you want every time, for example "end with one clear next step"].
Never: [what you never want, for example "hype words" or "promises about money"].

When I give you [what you will give it], you give me [what you want back].

Step 4: Give it a real task and read the result

Do not test it with something easy. Give it a real job from your week. Read the result out loud.

Step 5: Correct it once, and save the correction

When something does not sound like you, tell it, and ask it to remember the fix. Here is the exact line:

That line does not sound like me. I would say it like this: [your version].
Add that as a permanent rule in your instructions.

After a week of small corrections, you will read a draft and forget you did not write it.

A full example: a "Post Writer" AI employee

Here is a made-up example, so you can see what a finished job description looks like:

You are my Post Writer. Your one job is to turn my rough notes into a short post for my readers.

Who I help: women over 50 who want to start a small online business.
How I talk: short sentences, plain words, kitchen-table talk. I do not use hype words.
Always: start with something real, give one useful idea, end with one clear next step.
Never: promise income, use guru talk, or write more than 150 words.

When I give you rough notes, you give me one post I can read and approve.

Use the same pattern for any job. Change the words in brackets and you have a new AI employee.

Your first seven days

  1. Day 1: Pick the job.
  2. Day 2: Do the short interview so Claude learns who you are.
  3. Day 3: Write the job description with the template above.
  4. Day 4: Give it a real task.
  5. Day 5: Correct what does not sound like you, and save the fixes.
  6. Day 6: Use it for one real thing in your week.
  7. Day 7: Look at what worked. Then, only then, think about a second employee.

Common mistakes

  • Giving it five jobs at once. It does one job well and five jobs badly.
  • Skipping the brain file. Without it, everything sounds like every other AI post.
  • Never correcting it. Every correction makes it better. A helper you never correct never learns.
  • Posting without reading. Always read it first. You are the last step.

Frequently asked questions

Do I need to pay for this?

You can start with a free plan and see how it feels. Check the current plans on claude.com/pricing. Paid plans offer more room to work, but you do not need them on day one.

Will an AI employee replace me?

No. It does the first pass. The choosing, the checking and the caring are still yours. That is what people want from you anyway.

How many do I need?

I use thirteen, but I built them one at a time. You can do a lot with just one or two.

Is it safe to put my business in there?

Do not paste passwords, bank details or private information about other people. Keep it to your own words and your own business ideas.

What if it gives me something wrong?

It will, sometimes. That is why you read everything, and why you check any facts that matter.
