AI for Beginners Over 50: Where to Start (No Tech Skills Needed)

If AI feels like a club you were not invited to, this is for you. No tech words, no rush, just a calm place to start.

By Josée Bédard · November 23, 2026

First, take a breath

You are not behind. You do not need to know code. You do not need to understand how it works inside, just like you do not need to understand an engine to drive a car.

All you need to do is type, in plain words, like you are texting a friend.

What AI actually is, in plain words

An AI helper, like Claude, is a program you talk to by typing. You ask for something. It writes back. You can ask it to explain, to write a first draft, to make a list or to help you think.

Think of it as a very fast helper who has read a lot, but who does not know you and can make mistakes. You are the boss. You decide what is good.

What it can do for you

  • Explain things simply, in words you choose.
  • Write a first draft of an email, a letter or a post.
  • Make lists and plans, like a weekly schedule.
  • Tidy messy notes into something clear.
  • Give you ideas when you feel stuck.

What it cannot do (be careful here)

  • It can be wrong. It sometimes states things with confidence that are not true. Always check facts that matter, like dates, health and money.
  • It does not know you. Until you tell it, it does not know your story or what you want.
  • It is not a doctor, lawyer or financial advisor. Use it to understand, not to decide for you.
  • It does not do the work for you with no input. You still read it, fix it and approve it.

Staying safe

This matters, so I will say it plainly:

  • Do not paste passwords, bank details or ID numbers into any AI helper.
  • Do not share private information about other people.
  • Be careful of scams. If anyone promises easy money with AI, that is a warning sign.
  • Use the real website. Type the address yourself rather than clicking a link in a message.

How to get started

  1. Go to claude.com and make an account. There is a free plan to start with, and you can check current plans at claude.com/pricing.
  2. Open a new chat. You will see a box to type in.
  3. Type your first request, like the ones below.

The simple way to ask: four parts

Good requests have four parts. Here is the pattern:

Who I am: [a line about you]
What I want: [the task]
Details: [anything it should know]
How it should look: [short, a list, simple words]

You do not have to use all four every time. The more you give, the better it does.

Five things to try today (copy and paste)

1. Make a plan for your week

I am [your age] and I have these things to do this week: [list them messily]. Please turn this into a simple plan, one day at a time, in short sentences.

2. Explain something in plain words

Please explain [a thing you want to understand] in simple words, like you are talking to a smart friend who does not know the topic. Keep it short.

3. Write a first draft of a message

Help me write a kind, short message to [who] about [what]. Keep it warm and simple. Give me two versions to choose from.

4. Make a checklist

Make a simple checklist for [a task, for example getting ready for a trip]. Keep each step to one line.

5. Brainstorm

I want to [your goal]. Give me ten ideas, one line each, and mark the three that seem easiest to start.

How to get better answers

  • Ask again. If the first answer is not right, say what is wrong and ask for a new version.
  • Say what you want changed. "Shorter", "simpler" and "warmer" all work.
  • Tell it who you are. This is the biggest upgrade. I explain how in What Is a Brain File?.
  • Read everything. You are the last check.

Your first seven days

  1. Day 1: Make an account and say hello. Type "Hello, I am new to this. Can you help me get started?"
  2. Day 2: Try the weekly plan prompt.
  3. Day 3: Ask it to explain one thing you have wondered about.
  4. Day 4: Write one message with its help.
  5. Day 5: Make a checklist for something real.
  6. Day 6: Brainstorm ideas for something you care about.
  7. Day 7: Look back. What was most useful? Do more of that.

Common beginner mistakes

  • Giving up after one odd answer. Ask again, with more detail.
  • Trusting everything. Check what matters.
  • Sharing private details. Keep it to your own words and ideas.
  • Trying everything at once. Use it for one small thing first.
